Ljubljana:
Slovenia's green capital

8 min read  ·  Three-day itinerary

A small capital that feels like a big village: willow-lined riverbanks, café terraces spilling onto the cobbles, a hilltop castle keeping watch, and the Julian Alps on the horizon. Ljubljana is one of Europe's most walkable and liveable cities, a place where the car-free old town belongs to cyclists and strollers, and where an afternoon by the river is as worthwhile as any museum.

Why Ljubljana?

Ljubljana owes much of its look to one man: the architect Jože Plečnik, who spent the interwar years reshaping his home city with bridges, colonnades, markets, and riverside embankments. His work is so central to the city's character that it is now inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Add a cluster of Baroque churches, Viennese Secession façades rebuilt after the 1895 earthquake, and a pastel-coloured old town hemmed in by the curving Ljubljanica river, and you have a city that photographs beautifully from every angle.

It is also unusually green. Ljubljana was named European Green Capital in 2016, its centre has been largely car-free since 2007, and Tivoli Park runs almost to the doorstep of the old town. With a population under 300,000 and a large student community, the mood is relaxed and young; the riverside bars fill up on warm evenings, and the city makes an ideal base for day trips to Lake Bled, the Postojna caves, and the Soča valley.

Practical tip

The old town is compact and pedestrianised, so walking is the best way to get around. For longer hops, the BicikeLJ bike-share has stations across the city and the first hour of each ride is free once you register. Electric "Kavalir" buggies shuttle people around the car-free centre for free. The funicular up to the castle takes about a minute; the footpath from Vodnikov trg takes around fifteen.

The landmarks

Start at Prešeren Square, the city's meeting point, overlooked by the salmon-pink Franciscan Church of the Annunciation and a statue of France Prešeren, Slovenia's national poet. From here Plečnik's Triple Bridge fans out over the river into the old town, where Mestni trg and Stari trg are lined with Baroque townhouses, small boutiques, and the Town Hall with its Robba fountain.

Above it all sits Ljubljana Castle. Climb the viewing tower for a panorama of red roofs, church domes, and, on a clear day, the snow-capped Kamnik Alps. Back at river level, the Dragon Bridge, guarded by four copper dragons (the city's emblem), and Plečnik's colonnaded Central Market are both worth seeking out. Cross to the other side of town for Metelkova, a former army barracks turned into an autonomous cultural centre covered in murals and sculpture.

Ljubljana Castle

The medieval fortress crowning the hill above the old town. Take the funicular or walk up through the woods, then climb the outlook tower for the best view in the city. There are summer concerts and an open-air cinema in the courtyard.

Half-day visit

Triple Bridge & Old Town

Plečnik's three-span bridge links Prešeren Square to the cobbled lanes of Mestni trg and Stari trg. Wander slowly: the joy here lies in the courtyards, the river views, and the Baroque details above shop fronts.

Leisurely morning

Central Market

Open-air stalls of local produce, cheese, honey, and flowers beside Plečnik's riverside colonnade. Go on a weekday morning, then pick up a fried-fish cone from the Ribca stand under the arcades.

Morning stroll

Tivoli Park

Ljubljana's largest park, a short walk west of the centre. Jakopič Promenade hosts large-format outdoor photo exhibitions, and the trails climb gently into the forested Rožnik hill for a quiet escape from the city.

Quiet afternoon

When to go

Late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October) are ideal: warm days of 18–25°C, terraces open along the river, and fewer visitors than in high summer. July and August bring heat and the Ljubljana Festival, with concerts and theatre on open-air stages around the city, but also the biggest crowds and the highest hotel prices.

Winter is cold and often foggy, though December has its own charm. Festive lights are strung across the old town, and wooden market stalls line the riverbanks selling mulled wine and roasted chestnuts. January and February are the quietest months, perfect for museums and long lunches.

Where to stay

The old town and the streets around Prešeren Square put you within a few minutes' walk of everything, with boutique hotels in restored townhouses and apartments overlooking the river. Just north, the area around Slovenska cesta and Miklošičeva is more modern and close to the train and bus stations, which is handy for day trips. For something quieter and more local, look at Krakovo and Trnovo south of the centre: leafy residential lanes, riverside cafés, and an easy stroll into town.

Budget guide

Ljubljana is good value by Western European standards. Hostel dorms start at €25–35 per night; central boutique hotels run €100–180. A weekday set lunch (malica or kosilo) costs €10–15, and dinner at a good restaurant runs €30–50 per person. Daily budget excluding accommodation: €35–60 for modest spending; €80–120 including the castle, a river cruise, and wine bars.

Food & drink

Slovenian cooking blends Alpine, Mediterranean, Austro-Hungarian, and Balkan influences, and Ljubljana is the best place to sample all of them. Try štruklji, rolled dumplings filled with cottage cheese, tarragon, or walnuts; kranjska klobasa, the protected Carniolan sausage, served with mustard and fresh horseradish; and jota, a hearty bean and sauerkraut stew. For dessert, prekmurska gibanica layers poppy seed, walnut, apple, and cottage cheese into one rich slice.

On Fridays from spring to autumn, Open Kitchen (Odprta kuhna) takes over Pogačar Square next to the market, with dozens of local chefs cooking street-food portions. Slovenia is also a serious wine country: look for crisp whites from the Vipava valley and Goriška Brda, or orange wines from natural producers, all poured by the glass in the wine bars along the river.
